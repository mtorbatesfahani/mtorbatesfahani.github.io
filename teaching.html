Teaching

Student-centered engineering education that connects disciplinary knowledge with professional practice.

Teaching philosophy

My teaching is grounded in constructivist, student-centered learning that emphasizes active participation, collaboration, and reflection. As an engineering educator, my goal is to bridge fundamental knowledge and professional practice. I use real-world construction problems, case studies, hands-on activities, teamwork, and simulations to help students analyze evidence, evaluate alternatives, and make sound, practical engineering decisions.

I see my role as both educator and mentor. Through formative assessment, collaborative learning, open communication, industry engagement, and individual guidance, I work to create an inclusive environment in which students connect theory with practice and take ownership of their learning.

Teaching framework

Experience → Evidence → Judgment → Reflection

  1. ExperienceStudents encounter authentic design and construction problems.
  2. EvidenceThey investigate those problems using knowledge, data, and analysis.
  3. JudgmentThey make responsible engineering decisions.
  4. ReflectionThey reflect on outcomes to improve future practice.

Reflection informs the next experience.

Teaching approach in practice

In a construction project management course I taught, I used active and experiential learning in a weekly two-hour lab session. My classroom activities included:

Problem-based learning and case studies

Students analyze real construction challenges, such as safety hazards, cost overruns, and project delays, and propose data-supported solutions.

Collaborative projects

Teams simulate professional project environments, building leadership and communication skills.

Technology integration

Digital modeling tools and project management software help students visualize systems and evaluate design and construction alternatives.

Formative assessment and feedback

Quizzes, discussions, and peer review give continuous feedback, allow me to adjust my teaching, and keep activities aligned with explicit learning objectives.

Inclusive and reflective teaching

I aim to create a classroom in which all students feel valued, supported, and able to contribute, whatever their background or prior experience. Early each semester, I take time to understand my students’ academic goals, experiences, and motivations, and I emphasize collaboration, respect, and accessibility. I gather student feedback twice each semester, after the second class meeting and after the midterm, to identify areas for growth. After each semester, I review evaluations and refine my content delivery and engagement strategies.

Preparation in engineering education and mentoring

I completed the Graduate Certificate in Engineering Education at UTSA in Spring 2025. The nine-credit program covered engineering education methods and mentored teaching in engineering, emphasizing learner-centered and evidence-based pedagogy, assessment, inclusive teaching, reflection, curriculum design, and active-learning classroom management. I have also completed UTSA’s Graduate Mentoring Program and CIMER mentor training, which emphasize evidence-based, culturally aware mentoring: setting expectations, communicating effectively, fostering independence, and supporting student well-being.
