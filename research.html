Research vision

My research vision grew out of an interdisciplinary background in civil engineering, construction engineering, and construction management. My work has moved from safety education and workforce training to field-based studies of occupational health and safety, physiological and environmental monitoring, and wearable sensing. Increasingly, it uses technologies such as wearable sensing, artificial intelligence, machine learning, BIM, and digital twins to improve worker safety, worker well-being, and decision-making in construction.

I treat construction as a human-centered system, in which workers, technologies, environmental conditions, and project operations must be studied together rather than separately. My research program is organized around three interconnected domains. Technology innovation provides new ways to sense, monitor, visualize, and interact with construction environments. Safety and health research examines worker exposure, physiological response, behavior, training, and preventive interventions. Artificial intelligence and machine learning provide the analytical foundation for turning multimodal construction data into predictive, actionable information.

Integrated research cycle

Sense → Understand → Predict → Intervene → Evaluate

My research follows an integrated cycle in which each stage informs the next.

  1. SenseCollect physiological, environmental, and worker-reported data under real working conditions.
  2. UnderstandCharacterize exposure, physiological strain, and occupational risk, accounting for individual and contextual variability.
  3. PredictDevelop machine learning models that estimate strain, risk, and performance.
  4. InterveneTranslate evidence into training, work practices, and targeted, adaptive controls.
  5. EvaluateMeasure safety, health, and operational outcomes, and feed the results back into the next cycle.

Evaluation results return to Sense, so each cycle refines data collection, models, and interventions.

Domain 01

Technology Innovation in Construction

I study how sensing, digital modeling, and immersive technologies can capture what is happening on construction sites, and whether the workforce can understand and use them effectively.

Completed

  • Reviewed how virtual reality can strengthen workers’ responses to alerts from wearable sensing devices (Construction Research Congress 2024).
  • Examined drivers of, and barriers to, the use of wearable sensing devices for real-time risk assessment (Associated Schools of Construction, 2025).
  • Contributed, as a graduate research assistant, to a TRANS-IPIC project developing an IoT-driven digital twin framework for the design and fabrication of precast concrete elements.

Ongoing

  • Wearable sensing, IoT, and real-time monitoring. In my dissertation, I developed a field-deployable sensing framework that synchronizes physiological telemetry, environmental microclimate measurements, and worker-reported perceptions. The variability I observed among workers shows why individual and contextual information matters, rather than reliance on uniform exposure thresholds.
  • Multi-criteria assessment of commercially available wearable sensing devices for heat-stress monitoring (submitted manuscript).

Future directions

  • Multimodal sensing and IoT systems that integrate data from workers, environmental conditions, equipment, and project operations, including the synchronization, reliability, data quality, interoperability, and real-time communication of heterogeneous data streams in the field. Worker trust, technology acceptance, usability, and organizational readiness will be studied alongside technical performance.
  • Data-integrated BIM and digital twins that link physical construction processes with continuously updated digital representations, with applications in construction production, structural and infrastructure assembly, resource use, safety, and operational performance.
  • Immersive technologies and human–technology interaction. Interactive virtual environments, integrated with sensing, BIM, and digital twins, offer controlled settings for studying hazard recognition, information processing, response to digital alerts, situational awareness, and technology acceptance before field deployment.

Domain 02

Construction Safety and Health

I study how construction workers are exposed to occupational risk, how they respond, and which training and interventions measurably improve their safety and health.

Completed

  • Evaluated heat-stress prevention training for construction students and for the construction workforce, measuring changes in knowledge and health-related behavior.
  • Conducted a systematic review of heat-stress risk factors and prevention methods in construction.
  • Analyzed construction workers’ perceptions of heat-stress interventions and the influence of demographic factors.
  • Co-authored a structured review of suicide causal factors and mitigation strategies in the construction industry.
  • Supported the delivery of OSHA Susan Harwood workplace safety and health training on extreme temperatures and heat-stress prevention for construction workers.

Ongoing

  • Worker health in the field. With active construction workers, I integrate heart rate, estimated core temperature, and perceived exertion with environmental conditions to evaluate structured work–rest, hydration, and cooling interventions. This work has produced field evidence of differences in physiological response between intervention strategies.
  • Field-based risk assessment. By synchronizing physiological and environmental measurements during active construction work, I observed meaningful differences between workers’ responses, which supports accounting for individual and contextual variability in occupational risk assessment.

Future directions

  • Safety education, training, and behavior: how training content, delivery, individual characteristics, and technology influence hazard recognition, risk perception, knowledge retention, decision-making, and safety behavior, and whether gains in the classroom carry over to the jobsite.
  • Dynamic risk assessment: data-informed methods that combine hazard identification, exposure assessment, task characteristics, worker response, and site conditions to complement established safety criteria and improve the timing and specificity of preventive action.
  • Worker health in three dimensions: physiological (heat strain, cardiovascular response, hydration, exertion, recovery), physical (ergonomic exposure, musculoskeletal risk, physical workload), and mental and cognitive (mental workload, stress, attention, situational awareness).

Domain 03

Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning in Construction

I develop models that turn multimodal construction data into interpretable predictions, with explainability and human-centered decision support built in from the start.

Completed

  • Co-authored a comparative analysis of machine learning techniques for building cooling load prediction (Journal of Building Pathology and Rehabilitation, 2024).

Ongoing

  • Predictive modeling of physiological strain. Using wearable-sensor and environmental data collected in my dissertation, I am developing prototype models that characterize and predict physiological strain among construction workers, including an explainable machine learning framework for the early prediction of heat strain (submitted manuscript).

Future directions

  • Multimodal predictive analytics that combine physiological, environmental, behavioral, equipment, operational, and project-level data, using supervised, unsupervised, and deep learning methods.
  • Explainable and human-centered AI: model interpretability, feature attribution, uncertainty communication, transparency, trust, and how interface design affects appropriate reliance on AI.
  • AI-enabled decision support that uses model outputs and context to evaluate alternative responses and support decisions by workers, safety professionals, and project managers.

Contributions and funding

Contributions to knowledge

I aim to advance understanding of how workers, technologies, environmental conditions, and construction operations interact; how multimodal data can improve risk and performance assessment; and how human-centered AI can support prediction, explanation, and decision-making.

Contributions to practice

I aim to translate these advances into practical approaches for real-time monitoring, safety training, occupational risk assessment, worker health protection, digital-twin applications, and AI-supported decision-making, validated under realistic construction conditions and evaluated by measurable safety, health, and operational outcomes. Collaboration with contractors, safety professionals, technology providers, and other industry stakeholders is central to this aim.

Target funding agencies

My research aligns with programs supported by the National Institute for Occupational Safety and Health (NIOSH), the Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA), the Mine Safety and Health Administration (MSHA), the Center for Construction Research and Training (CPWR), and the Association for Iron & Steel Technology (AIST) for occupational safety, worker health, and training research; and with the National Science Foundation (NSF) and the U.S. Department of Transportation (USDOT) for intelligent construction technologies, sensing, digital twins, and AI-enabled systems.
